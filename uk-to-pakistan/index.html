    What actually decides the cost on this route

    The UK is one of the largest remittance corridors into Pakistan, and it is the route where the choice of provider makes the biggest difference to what your family receives. Two things drive that difference, and only one of them is advertised.

    The first is the fee, which providers display prominently and often discount to zero. The second is the exchange-rate margin — the gap between the wholesale rate a provider pays for rupees and the rate it quotes you. That margin is invisible, unlimited, and on a £500 transfer it is routinely worth several times more than the fee. A “no fee” transfer with a poor rate can easily deliver fewer rupees than a transfer with a £3 charge.

    This is why the table above ranks by rupees delivered rather than by headline price. It is the only comparison that reflects what your family can actually withdraw.

    Bank transfer: usually the cheapest way

    For a deposit straight into a Pakistani bank account, providers that charge their margin openly as a flat fee and convert at the mid-market rate normally come out ahead. There is no cash to handle, no agent network to pay, and the whole transfer is electronic from end to end.

    How you fund the transfer matters too. Paying from a UK bank account is normally the cheapest route. Debit card funding is faster to set up but most providers add a surcharge to cover card processing, and paying by credit card can be worse still — some UK card issuers treat a money transfer as a cash advance, which attracts interest immediately with no interest-free period.

    Cash pickup: the one case where the ranking flips

    If your family collects the money in person rather than into a bank account, the comparison changes completely. Wise does not offer cash pickup in Pakistan — it pays into bank accounts only. Switch the calculator above to Cash pickup and Wise disappears from the table entirely, leaving Remitly and WorldRemit to compete.

    Cash pickup also carries a genuine cost premium. Somebody has to staff a branch, hold physical rupees and verify the collector’s identity, and that cost is recovered through a higher fee, a worse rate, or both. If the recipient can use a bank account, they will nearly always receive more.

    Easypaisa and JazzCash from the UK

    Mobile wallet payouts sit between the two. The recipient needs no bank account — just a Pakistani mobile number registered to a wallet — and money typically lands within minutes. Pricing usually falls a little short of a bank deposit but comfortably ahead of cash collection.

    This is often the practical answer for sending to a parent or sibling in a smaller town, where a bank branch may be inconvenient but a wallet agent is not.

    Timing: when the money actually arrives

    Bank deposits generally complete within a few hours to one working day. Cash and wallet payouts are frequently available within minutes. The variable that catches people out is the funding leg: a transfer initiated on a Friday evening or over a UK bank holiday may not have its funds cleared until the next working day, which delays everything downstream regardless of how fast the payout side is.

    If a payment is time-critical — a medical bill, a school deadline — start it on a weekday morning rather than paying extra for an express option to compensate for a weekend.

    Regulation, limits and verification

    Firms providing money remittance to UK customers must be authorised or registered with the Financial Conduct Authority. That is not the same protection as bank deposit insurance — customer funds are safeguarded rather than FSCS-protected — but it does mean the firm is supervised, and you can check any provider’s status on the FCA register before sending.

    Expect identity verification, especially on your first transfer or on larger amounts. Providers are required to know who is sending money and, for larger sums, why. Having proof of identity and proof of address ready avoids a transfer being held mid-flight.

    Four ways to pay less on this route

    • Compare on rupees delivered, never on the fee. The fee is the small number. The rate is the big one.
    • Send fewer, larger transfers. Where a fee is fixed, one £600 transfer costs less than three of £200. Where a fee is waived above a threshold, crossing it is free money.
    • Fund from your bank account, not a card. The surcharge is avoidable.
    • Use a bank deposit where the recipient can accept one. The premium for cash collection is real and recurring.

    UK to Pakistan: common questions

    What is the cheapest way to send money from the UK to Pakistan?

    For a bank deposit, a provider that charges a visible flat fee and converts at the mid-market rate normally delivers the most rupees. For cash pickup the answer is different, because Wise cannot complete that transfer at all. Use the calculator above with your real amount and payout method rather than relying on a single fixed answer — the winner genuinely changes.

    How long does a UK to Pakistan transfer take?

    Bank deposits usually complete within a few hours to one working day. Cash pickup and mobile wallet payouts are often ready within minutes. Weekends and UK bank holidays slow the funding leg, which delays everything after it.

    Is a debit card or bank transfer cheaper for funding?

    A bank transfer from your UK account is normally cheaper. Card funding is quicker but usually carries a surcharge, and a credit card may be treated as a cash advance by your issuer — which adds interest from day one.

    Are these providers regulated in the UK?

    Yes. Payment and money remittance firms serving UK customers must be authorised or registered with the Financial Conduct Authority, and you can verify any provider on the FCA register. Note this is safeguarding, not FSCS deposit protection.

    Is there a limit on how much I can send to Pakistan?

    Providers set their own transfer limits, which vary by account verification level and payout method. Larger transfers typically require additional identity checks and sometimes evidence of the source of funds. Check the specific provider’s limits before committing to a deadline.
