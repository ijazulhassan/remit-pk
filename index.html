    How we work out the cost

    Advertised fees are the wrong thing to compare. A provider can charge nothing and still be the most expensive option on the page, because its margin is hidden in the exchange rate instead. So the calculator ignores marketing entirely and computes one number:

    Rupees delivered = (amount you send − fee) × that provider’s exchange rate

    Fees are modelled the way each provider actually charges — a fixed amount, a percentage, or nothing above a waiver threshold — so the ranking shifts with your amount rather than being fixed. Providers that do not support a payout method are removed from the table rather than shown with blanks.

    We are not a money transfer service and never touch your funds. When you click through to a provider we may earn a referral commission, which is how the site pays for itself. It does not change the ranking: the order is produced by the calculation above, and a provider cannot buy a higher position.

    Common questions

    Why do exchange rates differ between providers?

    Every provider buys currency at roughly the same wholesale “mid-market” rate, then adds a margin before quoting you. Some show that margin openly as a flat fee and pass you the real rate. Others fold it into a worse exchange rate so the transfer looks fee-free — you still pay, it is just harder to see. That is exactly why this page compares rupees delivered rather than advertised fees.

    Can I use Wise for cash pickup in Pakistan?

    No. Wise only pays into Pakistani bank accounts — it has no cash collection network in Pakistan. Switch the calculator to Cash pickup and it drops out of the table, leaving the providers that can actually complete that transfer.

    Is it safe to send money through these apps?

    Wise, Remitly and WorldRemit are all regulated money transfer businesses in the countries where they operate, each processing millions of transfers a year. They are not banks, but they are licensed and supervised specifically for cross-border payments.

    Bank transfer, cash pickup or mobile wallet — which is cheapest?

    Bank transfer, nearly always, because the provider has no cash-handling cost to recover. Mobile wallets like Easypaisa and JazzCash sit close behind and reach recipients without a bank account. Cash pickup costs the most but puts money in someone’s hand the same day, anywhere.

    How often are these numbers updated?

    Regularly, but not live. Exchange rates move continuously throughout the day, so treat these figures as a reliable guide to who is currently ahead rather than as a quote. Confirm the exact amount on the provider’s site before sending.
