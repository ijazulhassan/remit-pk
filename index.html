    The three corridors, in plain terms

    United Kingdom → Pakistan

    The UK is the largest remittance corridor into Pakistan outside the Gulf, and the one where provider choice matters most. Wise typically wins on straight bank deposits because it converts at the true mid-market rate and charges its margin openly as a flat fee. The catch: Wise does not offer cash pickup in Pakistan, so if your family collects in person it isn’t an option — switch the toggle above to Cash pickup and it drops out of the table entirely.

    For cash and mobile wallet payouts, Remitly and WorldRemit compete directly. Remitly’s Express option can land funds in minutes but prices that speed into the rate; its Economy option is slower and closer to Wise on value.

    UAE → Pakistan

    The tightest margins of the three. The Gulf corridor is fiercely contested — exchange houses, banks and app-based providers all chase the same workers sending money home monthly, so the spread between best and worst is narrower than on the UK or US routes. That doesn’t make comparison pointless: promotional rates rotate constantly, and small per-transfer gaps still add up to a real number across twelve transfers a year.

    Mobile wallet payouts — Easypaisa and JazzCash — have grown fastest here, since the recipient needs no bank account and the money arrives on a phone within minutes.

    United States → Pakistan

    US transfers split cleanly by purpose. For large, occasional amounts — university tuition, property, a family emergency — the exchange-rate margin dwarfs the fee, so a transparent mid-market provider saves the most in absolute rupees. For smaller monthly support payments where arrival speed matters more than the last few hundred rupees, an instant card-funded transfer can be worth the trade-off.

    Watch the funding method too: paying by debit or credit card is quicker but usually carries a surcharge that a direct bank debit avoids.

    Common questions

    Why do exchange rates differ between providers?

    Every provider buys currency at roughly the same wholesale “mid-market” rate, then adds a margin before quoting you. Some show that margin openly as a flat fee and pass you the real rate. Others fold it into a worse exchange rate so the transfer looks fee-free — you still pay, it is just harder to see. That is exactly why this page compares rupees delivered rather than advertised fees.

    Why does Wise disappear when I pick cash pickup?

    Because Wise only pays into Pakistani bank accounts — it has no cash collection network in Pakistan. Rather than show it with an empty row, the table removes it and ranks the providers that can actually complete that transfer.

    Is it safe to send money through these apps?

    Wise, Remitly and WorldRemit are all regulated money transfer businesses in the countries where they operate, each processing millions of transfers a year. They are not banks, but they are licensed and supervised specifically for cross-border payments.

    Bank transfer, cash pickup or mobile wallet — which is cheapest?

    Bank transfer, nearly always, because the provider has no cash-handling cost to recover. Mobile wallets like Easypaisa and JazzCash sit close behind and reach recipients without a bank account. Cash pickup costs the most but puts money in someone’s hand the same day, anywhere.

    How often are these numbers updated?

    Regularly, but not live. Exchange rates move continuously throughout the day, so treat these figures as a reliable guide to who is currently ahead rather than as a quote. Confirm the exact amount on the provider’s site before sending.
